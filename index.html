Featured project

Sex emerges at scale

The central claim is that sex and recombination need not be explained as exceptional biological add-ons. Natural selection itself can generate the negative associations that make genetic mixing advantageous, and at large scales the resulting selective response is dominated by rare, near-critical trajectories.

This program connects covariance, heavy-tailed sex potential, path ensembles, and nonequilibrium statistical mechanics. It also asks why non-additive fitness becomes progressively less consequential under coarse-graining, making universal structure visible above the microscopic details.

Main preprint Part 1 Part 2
  • Key idea: natural selection creates the associations that favor recombination.
  • Mathematical signature: sex potential has a heavy tail, with rare trajectories contributing disproportionately.
  • Broader aim: place the evolution of sex within a general statistical mechanics of adaptation.

Current questions

Where universal structure may make evolution predictable

01

Sex emerges at scale

Sex and recombination are usually treated as special biological mechanisms in need of special explanations. I am studying whether their selective advantage instead emerges as a generic, scale-dependent consequence of natural selection itself.

Current themes: covariance, path ensembles, critical trajectories, heavy-tailed sex potential, non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, and the coarse-graining of non-additive fitness.

02

Horizontal gene transfer

Antibiotics enrich resistance plasmids, but do not by themselves create direct selection for horizontal transfer. Under vertical transmission, linkage between host and plasmid fitness together with ongoing adaptation can generate antagonistic host–plasmid associations and hence selection for conjugation and other forms of HGT.

Current themes: antibiotic-resistance plasmids, host–plasmid covariance, HGT pressure, multiscale transmission, and preemptive risk forecasting.

03

Predictive evolution

Evolution is contingent at the microscopic level, but some coarse-grained observables are robust. The goal is to identify quantities that can be updated in real time and used for forward prediction without reconstructing the full genotype-to-fitness map.

Current themes: distributions of fitness effects, Bayesian updating, extreme-value structure, real-time forecasting, and prediction from low-dimensional summaries.

Working principle

Prediction without microscopic omniscience

Evolutionary detail is high-dimensional and contingent. Prediction becomes more plausible when we ask which coarse-grained quantities are stable to those details.

About

Mathematical biology across scales

I work at the interface of population genetics, probability, and statistical mechanics. Much of my research asks whether apparently idiosyncratic evolutionary processes contain coarse-grained regularities that can be measured, explained, and eventually used for prediction.

My work has included mutation-rate evolution, clonal interference, genetic linkage, distributions of fitness effects, real-time evolutionary forecasting, recombination, horizontal gene transfer, and microbial evolution.

I received my PhD in Zoology from Michigan State University, where I worked with Richard Lenski, after earlier training in biosystems engineering, physics, and philosophy.
